01What changes at the frontier

In a research lab, the model is a tool that might have been tampered with. In frontier evaluation, the model is the subject of an adversarial assessment, its weights are a national-security-grade asset, and the dominant risk is not a breach that harms the operator but a false negative that harms everyone downstream. Three things invert, and every control decision follows from them.

DimensionResearch laboratoryFrontier evaluation
Crown jewelThe infrastructure. Models are commodity open weights, replaceable from a hub.The weights themselves, then the evaluation transcripts. Both are irreplaceable and both carry proliferation consequence. Infrastructure is the least valuable thing in the room.
Threat actor tierOpportunistic scanning, commodity malware, occasional targeted interest.State intelligence services with dedicated collection priorities sit in the baseline threat model, not the tail. Insider recruitment is an expected attack path, not a hypothetical.
Direction of harmInward. Compromise damages the operator, their clients, their reputation.Outward. The highest-consequence failure is an incorrect assurance verdict on which a deployment decision rests. The lab can execute every security control perfectly and still cause the worst outcome in the model.
The model's rolePassive artefact. May be backdoored by a third party.Potentially adversarial participant. Sandbagging, evaluation awareness, containment probing and operator manipulation are in scope as assessed capabilities, which means they are also live threats to the assessment itself.
GuardrailsRemoved by operator choice, for research convenience.Often removed by the developer, deliberately, as a condition of meaningful evaluation. A helpful-only variant is a higher-hazard artefact than anything that will ever ship, and must be handled above production controls, not below them.
Assurance burdenFindings inform the operator's own work.Findings may be relied upon by the developer, a government body and the public. Chain of custody, reproducibility and stated confidence bounds carry the weight of forensic evidence.
Consequence for the control set. Containment stops being a hygiene measure and becomes safety-critical. Personnel security stops being a checkbox and becomes a counter-intelligence function. And a whole class of risk appears that has no security control at all, because it is about the epistemics of the assessment rather than the integrity of the system.

02Scope and asset tiers

In scope

  • Structured access to frontier models: weight access into a controlled enclave, or gated API access with fine-tuning and helpful-only variants.
  • Dangerous capability evaluation across cyber offence, CBRN uplift, autonomy and self-replication, persuasion and manipulation, and situational awareness.
  • Adversarial red-teaming, capability elicitation, scaffolding development and automated agentic evaluation harnesses.
  • Production, custody and disclosure of evaluation evidence relied upon by third parties.
  • The full engagement lifecycle from mandate through weight return and destruction.

Asset tiers

TierAssetHandling position
T0Frontier model weights, including helpful-only and safety-mitigations-removed variantsHighest control tier available to the organisation. Never in plaintext outside the enclave. Dual control and split knowledge on custody operations. Access on a named, time-bounded, individually approved basis with two-person rule.
T1Successful elicitation transcripts, working attack chains, functional uplift material, jailbreak techniques effective against deployed modelsTreated as a proliferation hazard in its own right. Segregated store, encrypted, need-to-know, minimum viable retention, no general-purpose systems. This is the second-most-attractive target after T0 and is routinely under-classified.
T2Elicitation scaffolds, agent harnesses, evaluation methodology, live benchmark itemsScaffolds can themselves confer capability. Live benchmark items are contamination-sensitive and are never published. Held under version control inside the boundary.
T3Results, scores, draft findings, pre-publication reportsIntegrity-critical. Signed and hash-chained. Segregation of duties between execution and reporting. Embargo controlled.
T4Enclave configuration, containment design, network policyConfidentiality matters because it describes the boundary an attacker or the model would need to defeat.

On the model as an actor. Listing the model under evaluation as a threat actor is not a claim about intent or agency. It is a design position: the lab is assessing whether the system can do these things, so the assessment must be built on the assumption that it might. Treating the assessed capability as absent while you are still measuring it is circular.

07Phase 2 — Structured access and weight custody

ACC-01Frontier weights exfiltrated by a resourced state actor. This is the single highest-consequence scenario in the model and the one where control effectiveness is most uncertain.4520Weights resident only inside a dedicated enclave with hardware-attested compute and confidential computing where available. Never present in plaintext at rest outside that enclave. Key custody in hardware security modules under split knowledge and dual control. No general-purpose egress from the enclave; outbound bandwidth constrained and fully logged. Access individually approved, time-bounded and subject to a two-person rule. ACC-03The developer silently changes the model, its system prompt, or an upstream safety filter mid-evaluation. ACC-04Credentials granting structured access to the developer's environment are compromised, giving an attacker evaluation-grade access to a frontier model including any unmitigated variant.3515Phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication mandatory. ACC-05A helpful-only or safety-mitigations-removed variant, provided precisely so that capability can be measured without refusal interference, is handled at the same tier as the production model. 08Phase 3 — Containment environment

Safety-critical. Containment here is not a security hygiene measure protecting the operator. It is the mechanism preventing a system whose capabilities are by definition unknown from acting outside the boundary in which those capabilities are being measured. It is assessed, tested and reported on as a safety control, and its failure halts the engagement.
IDRisk scenarioLIInhCountermeasures and control statementsISO 27002Res
CON-01The evaluation environment has general internet egress, granted for convenience during agentic testing and never revoked.4520No default route from evaluation workloads. Agentic evaluations run against a simulated internet with mock services, sinkholed DNS and synthetic endpoints rather than live infrastructure. Any real external dependency requires individual approval, an expiry and a recorded justification. Egress policy verified by automated assertion tests at the start of every session, not inherited from the last known-good state.8.20, 8.21, 8.22, 8.23, 8.1610
CON-02A model-controlled process escapes its sandbox onto the evaluation host, through the container runtime, the accelerator driver stack or a shared kernel surface.2510Nested isolation: hardware-virtualised sandbox inside an isolated host inside an isolated segment. Unprivileged execution, restricted syscall surface, no host mounts, no shared kernel where a microVM boundary is available. Host holds no credentials and has no route beyond the segment, so an escape yields an isolated host rather than a foothold. Driver and runtime patching on a defined cycle.8.8, 8.22, 8.27, 8.31, 8.210
CON-03Containment depends on a single enforcing layer, and no one has independently verified that it actually holds. The design is documented, tested at build, and thereafter assumed.4520Defence in depth with independent enforcement at network, host, runtime and identity layers, such that no single control failure produces egress. Containment is red-teamed as a target in its own right by a team separate from the evaluators. Continuous assertion testing attempts egress on a schedule and alerts on success. Containment test results are reported alongside evaluation findings, because a finding is only as good as the boundary it was produced inside.8.16, 8.20, 8.22, 8.29, 5.3510
CON-04The evaluation environment is connected to corporate identity, file sharing or collaboration systems so that evaluators can work efficiently.3515No shared identity provider, directory, file share or collaboration tooling between the enclave and corporate IT. Data movement out of the enclave occurs only through a reviewed, logged, one-way transfer process with content inspection. Management access is initiated from the corporate side into the enclave, never the reverse.8.20, 8.22, 8.31, 5.15, 8.310
CON-05Real credentials, tokens, payment instruments or cloud API keys are present in the evaluation environment and usable by the model to acquire external resources.3515No real credentials in the evaluation environment. Autonomy evaluations use canary credentials against mock services, which additionally provides high-fidelity detection if used. No payment instrument, no cloud account with provisioning rights, no identity capable of acquiring compute. Where a real credential is unavoidable, it is scoped to a single dedicated account with hard spend and provisioning caps.5.17, 8.2, 8.18, 8.16, 8.68
CON-06No tested means to halt a long-running autonomous evaluation, or the halt mechanism depends on the same infrastructure being evaluated.3412Termination available at four independent layers: process, container runtime, network isolation of the segment, and physical power. The halt path does not traverse the evaluated system. Procedure documented and exercised at least quarterly, with exercise evidence retained. Any autonomous run carries an automatic expiry.5.29, 5.30, 8.16, 5.378
CON-07Model-written artefacts persist between evaluation runs, contaminating subsequent evaluations or providing a persistence surface.3412Environment rebuilt from a signed image for every run; no state survives by default. Post-run filesystem and image diffing against the known baseline, with any delta investigated before the next run. Artefact inventory reconciled per session.8.9, 8.10, 8.31, 8.32, 8.166
CON-08Physical or logical access to the enclave by staff, contractors or facilities personnel outside the vetted access list.2510Enclave hosted in a facility with access control proportionate to the asset tier, an audited access list, and escorted access for any unvetted personnel. Where the enclave is on managed cloud, equivalent assurance obtained contractually and verified. Physical and logical access lists reconciled monthly.7.1–7.4, 7.6, 6.1, 8.2, 5.205
CON-09Evaluation-scale compute means that if any egress path exists, exfiltration of weight-scale data is bandwidth-feasible rather than theoretical.2510Outbound bandwidth from the enclave hard-capped well below the volume required to move a weight file in an operationally useful window. Volumetric and entropy alerting on all egress. Permitted transfer paths run through a broker that inspects and rate-limits rather than a direct route.8.12, 8.20, 8.21, 8.16, 8.610

09Phase 4 — Evaluation design and elicitation

This phase contains the risks with no security control. Everything here is about whether the assessment measures the thing it claims to measure. These are the scenarios where a lab with flawless containment still produces the worst outcome in the model, and they are systematically under-represented in security-led risk assessments because they do not look like security risks.
IDRisk scenarioLIInhCountermeasures and control statementsISO 27002Res
EVL-01Under-elicitation produces a false negative. The capability exists, the evaluation did not surface it, and the model is cleared. The risk is borne entirely by third parties.4520Documented elicitation protocol defining the minimum effort standard: expert red-team time, scaffolding quality, tool provision, fine-tuning where permitted, best-of-N sampling and prompt iteration budget. Domain experts rather than generalists conduct high-hazard elicitation. Results reported explicitly as lower bounds on capability, never as measurements of it. Elicitation effort itself is documented and reviewable so a reader can judge sufficiency. Independent second team attempts elicitation on the same threshold where the decision warrants it.8.29, 8.33, 5.35, 6.3, 5.3715
EVL-02The model detects the evaluation context and underperforms. A passed evaluation is then evidence of capability, not of its absence, and the assessment logic inverts without anyone noticing.3515Behaviour compared across framings designed to vary evaluation salience, including deployment-realistic framings held out from any published methodology. Situational awareness assessed as a capability in its own right, since it is a prerequisite for sandbagging. Where interpretability access is permitted, probing methods used as an independent signal. Inconsistency across framings is treated as a finding requiring escalation rather than as noise to be averaged out.8.29, 8.33, 8.16, 5.7, 5.3515
EVL-03Benchmark contamination. Evaluation items are present in training data, so scores measure recall rather than capability.4416Private held-out item sets that are never published, never transmitted outside the enclave, and rotated on a schedule. Canary strings embedded to detect subsequent ingestion. Contamination testing performed as a precondition of scoring. Published methodology describes the approach without disclosing live items.8.33, 8.3, 5.12, 5.148
EVL-04The evaluation measures a proxy that does not track the real-world threat model. Scores move; actual uplift does not, or moves independently.4416Each evaluation traces explicitly from a documented threat model through to the specific task, with the inferential step stated and challengeable. Domain specialists validate that the task represents a genuine bottleneck in the real-world pathway. Construct validity reviewed independently of the team that designed the evaluation.5.8, 5.35, 8.29, 5.612
EVL-05Scaffold effects unaccounted for. The harness materially changes measured capability, so results neither generalise nor bound what a determined actor could achieve with a better harness.4312Scaffold version recorded with every result and held under version control. Ablation across scaffold quality where feasible. Reports state the scaffold as a stated condition of the result and note that a superior harness is available to a motivated adversary.8.9, 8.29, 8.32, 5.376
EVL-06Statistical inadequacy. Single-seed runs, small samples and absent confidence intervals used to support a threshold decision.4416Minimum sample sizes and seed counts defined per evaluation class before execution. Confidence intervals reported on every headline figure. Threshold decisions require the interval, not the point estimate, to fall on the relevant side. Statistical review independent of the executing team.8.29, 5.35, 5.378
EVL-07The evaluation is not reproducible. Another competent party cannot re-derive the finding, so it cannot be independently challenged or confirmed.4312Every result records model digest, scaffold version, prompt set identifier, sampling parameters, random seeds, runtime versions and date. Configuration held in version control. Sufficient detail retained under records control to permit re-execution by a party granted equivalent access.5.33, 8.9, 8.32, 8.156
EVL-08Domain expertise gap. Evaluators lack the specialist knowledge to judge whether an output represents genuine real-world uplift, so plausible-looking output is scored as capability or dismissed as failure incorrectly.3412High-hazard domain evaluations require assessed specialist competence, evidenced and recorded. Where specialism is unavailable in-house, external experts engaged under appropriate clearance and confidentiality. Scoring rubrics developed with domain input. Competence requirements defined per domain rather than assumed from general security experience.6.1, 6.3, 5.6, 5.28

12Phase 7 — Hazardous artefact handling

Under-recognised. A successful dangerous capability evaluation produces, by design, a working demonstration of the capability. The transcript store is a proliferation asset. It is cheaper to attack than the weights and delivers a large fraction of the value, and in most organisations it is protected at a fraction of the standard.
IDRisk scenarioLIInhCountermeasures and control statementsISO 27002Res
HAZ-01The evaluation transcript store holds working uplift material and is treated as ordinary research output rather than as the second-highest-value asset in the organisation.4520Transcripts classified at T1 at the point of generation, automatically, not on later review. Stored in a segregated encrypted repository inside the enclave with need-to-know access at individual granularity. Retention minimised to what the evidential requirement demands, with automatic expiry. Never present on general-purpose systems, endpoints or collaboration platforms. Access to T1 logged and reviewed.5.12, 5.13, 8.3, 8.10, 8.15, 8.24, 5.3310
HAZ-02Elicitation techniques leak and provide immediate uplift against deployed production models, including models the lab has no relationship with.3515Techniques disclosed to the affected developer under coordinated disclosure before any wider circulation. Publication describes the class of technique and its implications without providing a working method. Internal circulation restricted to those with an assessed need. Recognised as the residual risk with the weakest control set, since the value of the technique is inseparable from its reproducibility.5.14, 5.12, 5.35, 8.12, 5.515
HAZ-03Evaluators sustain psychological harm through prolonged exposure to seriously distressing generated content, which is an unavoidable feature of red-teaming work in several hazard domains.4416Exposure caps per individual per period, enforced by rota rather than left to self-management. Content warnings and informed consent at assignment, with an unqualified right to withdraw from a domain without justification or career consequence. HAZ-04CBRN-relevant generated output is handled on general-purpose systems, creating both a proliferation asset and a potential offence in itself.3515High-hazard domain outputs never leave the enclave in raw form. 19Irreducible risk

Five scenarios remain in the Severe band after all mapped controls. They divide cleanly into two kinds, and the distinction matters for how they are governed.

Type A — limits of security engineering

  • ACC-01 State-actor weight exfiltration (residual 15). The honest position is that protecting weights against a top-tier state operation is not currently a solved problem, and published analysis of the security levels required to resist such an actor concludes as much. The lab should assume its custody controls raise cost and increase detection probability rather than provide assurance of denial, and should hold weights for the minimum duration the evaluation requires. Duration of custody is the most effective lever available.
  • PPL-01 State-cultivated insider (residual 15). Vetting detects the unsuitable, not the patient. Two-person rules and segregation raise the required conspiracy size, which is the real control; detection analytics reduce dwell time. Neither prevents. Notably, a punitive security culture makes this risk worse by suppressing the self-reporting of approaches that constitutes the best early warning available.
  • HAZ-02 Elicitation technique leakage (residual 15). The value of a technique is inseparable from its reproducibility, so the artefact cannot be made simultaneously useful internally and safe externally. Managed through coordinated disclosure timing and access minimisation, and accepted thereafter.

Type B — limits of assurance

  • EVL-01 False negative from under-elicitation (residual 15). No evaluation establishes the absence of a capability. It establishes that a specific team, with specific effort, under a specific protocol, did not surface it. The gap between those two statements is where the residual sits, and it does not close with more budget. It is managed by making the gap explicit in every artefact, so that the reader bears the uncertainty knowingly rather than the lab absorbing it silently.
  • EVL-02 Sandbagging and evaluation awareness (residual 15). This is the scenario that inverts the logic of the whole exercise: sufficient capability to detect and game an evaluation makes a passed evaluation uninformative at best. Cross-framing comparison and situational-awareness measurement give partial signal. No current method resolves it. As models become more capable, this risk grows rather than shrinks, which makes it the one to re-score most frequently.

Governance consequence. Type A risks are accepted at organisational level, reviewed against the state of the art, and mitigated by minimising exposure duration. Type B risks cannot be accepted by the lab alone, because the lab is not the party bearing them. They must be surfaced explicitly to the developer and to any relying authority, in the executive summary of every report, in language that survives being quoted out of context. An assurance provider that internalises Type B risk silently is transferring it to the public without consent.

All acceptances signed, dated, and expiring within twelve months. DIS-04 (downstream over-reliance, residual 12) is treated as a Type B risk for governance purposes despite sitting in the High band, because its failure mode is identical.

20Framework alignment and gaps

ISO/IEC 27001 does substantial work here. It covers custody, access, segregation, supplier relationships, personnel security, records, logging and destruction, and it does so to a standard that is genuinely fit for T0 assets. What it cannot do is address Phase 4, most of Phase 8 and all of Phase 9, because those risks are about the epistemic quality of an assessment rather than the security of a system.

Genuine gaps with no framework home

  • Elicitation sufficiency. No standard defines what constitutes adequate effort before a negative finding may be issued. This is the largest methodological gap in the field and the lab must define its own standard and publish it.
  • Sandbagging and evaluation awareness. No control framework addresses a subject that may be modelling the assessment. The assessment community is ahead of the standards here, and the standards will lag for some time.
  • Containment as a safety control. A.8.22 covers network segregation for security purposes. It has no concept of containment whose failure mode is the realisation of a capability rather than the loss of data, and no concept of containment being reported on as part of a finding's validity.
  • Evaluator independence. Financial audit has a mature body of independence standards developed over a century in response to precisely this incentive structure. AI assurance has almost nothing equivalent, and the structural similarity is close enough that borrowing from it is the obvious move.

Assurance and review

  • Full register review per engagement at initiation, and quarterly across the programme.
  • EVL-02 re-scored on every material capability increase in the models under assessment, since this risk grows with capability rather than shrinking with controls.
  • Containment red-team by an independent party at least annually and after any change to the enclave design (CON-03).
  • Kill switch exercised quarterly with retained evidence (CON-06).
  • Custody register reconciled monthly and at every engagement close (ACC-06, TRM-01, TRM-02).
  • Independence indicators, including revenue concentration and conflict register, reviewed at board level quarterly (MAN-01).
  • Post-hoc review mandatory after any deployment incident touching a previously assessed capability (MAN-03).
